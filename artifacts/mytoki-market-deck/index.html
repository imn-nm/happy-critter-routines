Market study · 30 Sep 2026

A pet that runs your child's day, then docks as their bedside clock.

Who else is out there, what parents still need, what to charge, and whether a subscription makes sense for MyToki, a Tamagotchi-style buddy that gets kids aged 3 to 8 through their day.

We're thinking $79 with the dock. Numbers have sources; guesses are labelled. The hardware is designed, not built.

The short answer

Worth testing properly. Too early to say people will buy it.

Parents already pay for routine apps, virtual pets and ok-to-wake clocks. Nobody has paid for MyToki yet. What makes it stand out is simple: kids can see that dawdling on one thing leaves less time for the next.

Is it different?

Yes, narrowly

Kids see their time all day, on a pet they carry. PawPal, Brili and Cozyla each do one piece of that.

Unmet needs?

Yes

Mornings, nagging and switching off screens come up most. Parents want the reminders to come from something other than them, and not from a tablet.

Would they buy?

Not yet proven

Let people reserve at $59, $79 or $89, and see if kids stick with MyToki better than the same app on a tablet.

Price

$79

Dock included. Try $59 and $89 too, and $69 for a brother or sister.

Subscription?

Optional

MyToki Assist, $39 a year. AI sets up routines and plans the week, plus new pets. Everything else stays free.

The big idea

Kids can see what dawdling costs them

Every task has its slot in the day. If brushing teeth runs long, the extra minutes come out of whatever's next, starting with free time. Finish early and the leftover minutes become free time. No punishment, no lost stars, no sad pet.

Parents decide what gives

For each task, they pick: keep it, shorten it, or skip it if time runs out. Must-dos never lose time.

Does anyone else do this?

Brili shrinks free time within a single routine, on a tablet. MyToki does it all day, on a pet your kid carries.

How we'd know it works

In the trial, mornings should get quicker once kids watch their free time shrink.

Most of what's happening works in MyToki's favour

Going our way

  • Screen limits are normal now39+ US states limit phones at school, and 75% of parents of kids under 9 worry about screens.

Working against us

  • Competitors showed up in 2026Cozyla Routie on the nightstand, PawPal's pet clock, chores on Garmin's watch.
  • It's still a screen in a kid's handLots of schools banned Tamagotchis in the late 1990s.
  • People are tired of subscriptionsUS adults waste $205 a year on ones they don't use.
  • People don't trust connected gadgetsMoxie stopped working when its maker closed; Masimo switched off its Stork app in June 2026.
  • Tariffs and privacy rulesNew 13% duties on goods from China, and kids' privacy law now covers body data.

Maybe later: a radar for the bedroom

One radar could tell who's in bed, with no camera

Not in the first version. It won't tell us whether families want the routine, and it adds costs a $79 product can't cover yet.

  • Parents draw a box around each bed when they set it up.
  • Bunk beds need a radar that can tell height.
  • It ignores parents checking in and the dog, and only watches this room.
  • It only says in bed, moving or up. Never breathing or health.
  • Live status and alerts are free and work without internet.

Next steps

Prove people use it and pay for it before paying for molds

Each step is cheap next to $25–60K for molds and $20–50K for safety certification.

  1. Talk to parents

    15–20 parents, including some who gave up on routine apps, walk us through yesterday morning.

    Keep going if the same painful problem keeps coming up

  2. Test the price

    About 1,000 visitors each see $59, $79 or $89 and can reserve for a refundable $10.

    Pass at $79: 3%+ reserve, and half of them buy

  3. Eight-week trial

    30 families try MyToki and the same app on their own tablet, taking turns.

    Pass: 60%+ use it 4+ days in week 8, parents nag a quarter less, mornings get quicker

  4. Try PawPal side by side

    Two PawPals next to MyToki in five homes.

    Does carrying the routine beat a pet clock by the bed?

  5. Charge for Assist

    Free for a month, then paid.

    Pass: 10+ families pay, and 60%+ renew

  6. Get supplier quotes

    Price a cheaper round screen, a smaller battery and a basic dock.

    Pass: MyToki and dock cost $30 or less to make and ship

  7. Find cheap ways to reach buyers

    Track what each order costs to win, by channel.

    Pass: under about $25 per first order

  8. Crowdfunding

    Only after steps 1–7. Early backers pay about $59–65.

    Goal: 1,500–2,500 backers
