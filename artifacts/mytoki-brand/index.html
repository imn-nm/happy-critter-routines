MyToki

One hop at a time.

MyToki is a round screen on a child's shelf with a pixel pet living inside. The pet walks them through the day one step at a time and cheers for every one. Biscuit the rabbit is the first pet, and more can move in. This book covers how MyToki looks, sounds and talks, for kids from 2 to 10.

Mine

It starts with my. The screen belongs to the child, not the grown-ups, and the logo is a pair of eyes peeking out of its round window.

Six letters, none of them silent. A kid who is learning to read can sound it out on the box.

The MyToki chime

Two soft notes going up: a G, then the C above it. It plays when a pet says hello and when a step is done. It never plays for a warning, so kids only ever hear it for good things.

The pets

Meet Biscuit, the first pet

Every MyToki pet is a little pixel animal that keeps the child company all day and falls asleep when they do. Biscuit is a cream rabbit with one ear that won't stay up. Pets are drawn in crisp pixels, exactly as the device renders them.

Pets always

  • Cheer when a step is done, however small.
  • Talk about now and next. The day just keeps moving.
  • Face the child to say hello or cheer, then turn back to what they're doing.
  • Keep their one special thing. For Biscuit that's the flopped ear, in every drawing and at every size.
  • Wear what the kid picks. Bows, caps, capes, all of it.

Pets never

  • Look sad, cross or worried, even when a step gets skipped.
  • Mention a missed step. There's no such thing on the child's screen.
  • Hurry anyone. The ring shows time passing, so the pet doesn't need to nag.
  • Wink. Both eyes blink together.
  • Get called a boy or a girl. Each pet has a name. When you need a pronoun, use they.

Rules for new pets

Same size

About 22 × 40 pixels facing front, on the same grid as Biscuit, so every pet fits the ring and the timer.

Same build

Square 4 × 4 eyes, chibi arms 6 × 5, no outline, and soft colors that read on navy. Same poses: hello, yay, busy, sleepy.

One special thing

Biscuit has the flopped ear. A cat might get a curly tail, a bear a round tummy. One thing, and it's always there.

Logo

The o is a window

MyToki is spelled in soft beads, like a craft-bead picture on a pegboard. The o is the round screen, with two eyes peeking out of the dark. It doesn't say which pet is inside, so it works for Biscuit and every pet after. The i wears a star.

  • A 25 × 14 bead grid. Each bead is a rounded square at 84% of its cell with a small shine at the top left.
  • The o is one bead taller than the other letters at the top and the bottom, the way round letters are in a good typeface.
  • The ring is one bead thick around a dark screen with two square eyes, like every pet's. On the hero they blink and look around.

Color rules

Mix warm and cool

Anything that uses friend colors mixes at least one warm (Coral, Carrot, Bubblegum) with one cool (Mint, Sky, Lavender). Then nothing reads as "for boys" or "for girls".

One lime per screen

Lime marks the single thing to press: Done, Next, Play. If two things are lime, neither stands out.

Amber means stars

Star Amber is only for stars and rewards, so kids learn that yellow-orange means something good from a grown-up.

Kids pick their color

Siblings each choose a friend color for their own screen. It's never handed out by gender.

Sentence case everywhere. Headlines end with a full stop or an exclamation mark, never both. Pixel labels can be all caps with a little extra letter spacing.

Voice

How MyToki talks

Like a friend who's doing it with you. Short, warm, and about right now. Every pet talks this way, and so does everything with MyToki on it.

Short

Five words or fewer on the child's screen. Pictures carry the rest.

Now and next

This step and the one after it. Never what was missed.

On their side

"Let's" instead of "you need to". The pet does it too.

Real words

Teeth, shoes, bath. Not task, routine or complete.

What's next for MyToki

This is a first pass, and the name is a working title. Before it goes further:

  • Biscuit keeps their name. Each new pet gets its own name, and MyToki stays the name of the whole thing.
  • Sketch the next pets on Biscuit's grid, following the rules for new pets.
  • Run a trademark search on MyToki in toy, app and children's electronics classes.
  • Try the chime and the face mark with a few kids at each end of the age range.
  • Swap the paw app icon and the PetPals name in the app for the MyToki ones.
